# Levels and Lines — Field Guide

How to estimate developmental levels, read psychographs, and translate a message so
that each level can hear it.

> Hold every estimate lightly. Levels describe *structures of meaning-making*, not the
> worth of a person. Everyone has access to a range, states can temporarily shift how
> people show up, and lines develop unevenly.

## 1. The altitude spectrum

| Altitude | Wilber's name | Spiral Dynamics | Kegan | Action logic (Torbert / Cook-Greuter) | Perspective | Org form (Laloux) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Infrared | Archaic | Beige | — | — | survival | — |
| Magenta | Magic | Purple | 1 (impulsive) | Impulsive | egocentric, tribal | Tribal clan |
| Red | Magic-Mythic | Red | 2 (imperial) | Opportunist | egocentric | Impulsive "wolf pack" |
| Amber | Mythic | Blue | 3 (socialized) | Diplomat / Expert | ethnocentric | Conformist "army" |
| Orange | Rational | Orange | 3→4 | Expert / Achiever | worldcentric (early) | Achievement "machine" |
| Green | Pluralistic | Green | 4→4/5 | Individualist / Pluralist | worldcentric | Pluralistic "family" |
| Teal | Integral (early vision-logic) | Yellow | 4/5→5 | Strategist | worldcentric → kosmocentric | Evolutionary "living organism" |
| Turquoise | Holistic (late vision-logic) | Turquoise | 5 | Alchemist / Construct-aware | kosmocentric | — |
| Indigo → Violet → Ultraviolet → Clear Light | Third tier (para-mind, meta-mind, overmind, supermind) | Coral (partial) | — | Unitive / Ironist | kosmocentric | — |

Correspondences are approximate. Different researchers slice the spectrum differently;
the colors are a shared ruler, not a precise measurement.

**First tier** (Infrared → Green): each level tends to believe its worldview is the
right one and the others are wrong. **Second tier** (Teal, Turquoise) sees that every
level is a necessary part of the spiral and asks what is *appropriate* here, rather than
what is right forever. Wilber also describes a **third tier** of transpersonal
structures, which are rarely relevant to everyday analysis.

## 2. Each level in practice

### Magenta — Magic (Purple)
- **Worldview:** the world is alive with spirits and signs; safety in the tribe; ritual
  and tradition keep us safe.
- **Healthy:** belonging, kinship, ancestral wisdom, ritual, deep bonds.
- **Unhealthy:** superstition, magical thinking, fear of outsiders.
- **Speaks to it:** stories, rituals, elders, safety, "our people".

### Red — Power (Red)
- **Worldview:** the world is a jungle; act now, take what you want, gain respect.
- **Healthy:** courage, energy, decisiveness, breaking free from oppression,
  entrepreneurial drive, heroic action.
- **Unhealthy:** domination, impulsiveness, exploitation, short-term thinking.
- **Speaks to it:** immediate rewards, respect, strength, clear consequences, heroes.

### Amber — Order (Blue)
- **Worldview:** there is one right way, given by a higher authority; follow the rules
  and sacrifice now for later reward.
- **Healthy:** discipline, loyalty, duty, reliability, stable institutions, moral
  clarity, process.
- **Unhealthy:** rigidity, dogmatism, us-vs-them, conformity, guilt and shame.
- **Speaks to it:** duty, tradition, rules, clear roles, "the right thing to do",
  respected authority.

### Orange — Achievement (Orange)
- **Worldview:** the world is a machine you can understand and master; progress through
  reason, science and competition; meritocracy.
- **Healthy:** innovation, science, prosperity, autonomy, strategy, measurable goals,
  individual rights.
- **Unhealthy:** materialism, burnout, exploitation of people and nature, "flatland"
  (only what can be measured matters), cynicism.
- **Speaks to it:** results, ROI, data, competitive advantage, personal success,
  efficiency.

### Green — Community (Green)
- **Worldview:** all people matter equally; feelings, inclusion and relationships come
  first; all views deserve a hearing.
- **Healthy:** compassion, inclusion, consensus, ecological care, diversity, human
  rights, sensitivity to the marginalized.
- **Unhealthy:** endless process, inability to decide, relativism ("no view is better"),
  hidden elitism against other levels, "mean green" (attacking all hierarchy, even
  healthy ones), what Wilber called *boomeritis*.
- **Speaks to it:** fairness, inclusion, authenticity, impact on people and planet,
  shared values, dialogue.

### Teal — Integral / Systemic (Yellow)
- **Worldview:** the world is a complex, evolving system; every perspective has a place;
  what matters is what fits this context now.
- **Healthy:** systems thinking, comfort with paradox and uncertainty, self-management,
  natural hierarchies of competence, purpose-driven work, low fear.
- **Unhealthy:** detachment, intellectual arrogance ("I'm more evolved"), analysis
  without engagement, coldness.
- **Speaks to it:** purpose, elegance, systemic leverage, learning, freedom,
  evolutionary potential.

### Turquoise — Holistic (Turquoise)
- **Worldview:** everything is interconnected in one living whole; intuition and
  reason, self and world are integrated.
- **Healthy:** global holistic care, deep presence, spontaneous ethics, unity with
  complexity.
- **Unhealthy:** spiritual inflation, vagueness, withdrawal from practical life.
- **Speaks to it:** wholeness, the planetary, the sacred in ordinary life.

## 3. Correspondence with other models

Useful when the user already knows one of them:

- **Piaget (cognition):** sensorimotor → preoperational → concrete operational
  (≈ Amber) → formal operational (≈ Orange) → post-formal / vision-logic (≈ Teal+).
- **Gebser (cultural structures):** archaic → magic → mythic → mental-rational →
  integral-aperspectival.
- **Maslow (needs):** physiological → safety → belonging → self-esteem →
  self-actualization → self-transcendence.
- **Kohlberg (moral):** pre-conventional (≈ Red) → conventional (≈ Amber) →
  post-conventional (≈ Orange/Green and beyond).
- **Fowler (faith):** intuitive-projective → mythic-literal → synthetic-conventional →
  individuative-reflective → conjunctive → universalizing.
- **Kegan (orders of mind):** socialized mind (shaped by others' expectations) →
  self-authoring mind (own internal compass) → self-transforming mind (can hold and
  revise multiple systems).

## 4. Reading levels from evidence

Infer levels from *how* people reason, not *what* they conclude. Two people can hold the
same opinion from very different levels.

| Cue | Amber-ish | Orange-ish | Green-ish | Teal-ish |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Source of authority | The rule, the book, the boss | Evidence, results | Consensus, lived experience | Context, what fits the whole |
| Mistakes are… | Sins / failures of duty | Inefficiencies to fix | Harms to relationships | Feedback for learning |
| Disagreement is… | Disloyalty | Competition | Something to include | Information |
| Hierarchy is… | Natural and sacred | Earned by merit | Suspect | Fine when it serves function |
| Time horizon | Tradition / afterlife | Quarters, careers | Generations, the planet | Evolutionary |
| Typical question | "What is the rule?" | "What works best?" | "Who is left out?" | "What is needed now?" |

Look for patterns across several cues. Note when someone shows different levels in
different contexts or lines.

## 5. Translating across levels

The same initiative must often be framed differently for different audiences. This is
not manipulation when the substance is honest: it is meeting people where they live.

Example — a new safety procedure in a factory:

- **Red:** "Follow this and nobody gets hurt on your shift. The crew that goes 90 days
  clean gets the bonus."
- **Amber:** "This is the official procedure. Everyone follows it, every time. It is our
  duty to each other."
- **Orange:** "This procedure cuts incidents by 40% and saves us downtime and cost.
  Here's the data."
- **Green:** "We care about every person going home whole. We built this procedure
  together with the floor team."
- **Teal:** "Safety is a property of the whole system. This procedure plus your
  judgment on the ground is how we stay adaptive."

**Rule of thumb:** communicate *to* the center of gravity of the audience, while leaving
a door open one level up.

## 6. Developmental lines

| Line | Core question | Researchers often cited |
|---|---|---|
| Cognitive | What am I aware of? How complex can I think? | Piaget, Commons & Richards |
| Self / ego identity | Who am I? | Loevinger, Cook-Greuter |
| Values | What is significant to me? | Graves, Spiral Dynamics |
| Moral | What should I do? | Kohlberg, Gilligan |
| Interpersonal | How should we interact? | Selman, Perry |
| Emotional | How do I feel about this? How well do I handle feelings? | Goleman |
| Needs | What do I need? | Maslow |
| Spiritual | What is of ultimate concern? | Fowler |
| Aesthetic | What is beautiful and attractive to me? | Housen |
| Kinesthetic / somatic | How do I physically do this? | — |
| Psychosexual | How do I relate to desire and intimacy? | — |
| Worldview | What is the world like? | Gebser |

Common patterns worth naming:

- **High cognitive, low interpersonal** — a brilliant founder whose team quits.
- **High spiritual states, low moral/interpersonal development** — the charismatic
  teacher who abuses power.
- **High emotional, low cognitive** — warmth without strategic clarity.
- **High values aspiration, low somatic** — idealism that burns out the body.

## 7. Psychograph template

Rate each relevant line from 1 (early) to 5 (advanced) for the person or team, as a
rough sketch only.

```
Line            1   2   3   4   5
Cognitive       ■   ■   ■   ■   □
Emotional       ■   ■   □   □   □
Interpersonal   ■   ■   □   □   □
Moral           ■   ■   ■   □   □
Somatic         ■   □   □   □   □
```

Then answer: Which line is the **leading edge**? Which is the **limiting factor**? What
is one practice that would raise the limiting factor (see
[integral-practice.md](integral-practice.md))?
