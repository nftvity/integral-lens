## Business strategy and organizations

| Quadrant | Look at |
|---|---|
| **I** | Leaders' and employees' purpose, motivation, mindsets, fears, beliefs about the market |
| **It** | Skills, behaviors, performance, productivity, the product itself |
| **We** | Culture, values in practice, trust, morale, informal power, the stories told about the company |
| **Its** | Strategy, structure, processes, incentives, technology, finances, market, regulation, supply chain |

**Typical blind spot:** strategy and restructuring (LR) without culture (LL) — "culture
eats strategy for breakfast". Also: leadership mindset (UL) is invisible in the plan.

**Organizational levels (Laloux):** Red (wolf pack, power) → Amber (army, hierarchy
and process) → Orange (machine, objectives and innovation) → Green (family,
empowerment and values) → Teal (living organism, self-management, wholeness,
evolutionary purpose). Most real organizations are mixed; diagnose by practices
(how decisions, hiring, compensation and conflict actually work).

## Leadership and teams

- **I:** leader's self-awareness, intentions, emotional regulation, developmental
  stage (action logic).
- **It:** observable leadership behaviors, meeting practices, decision speed,
  delegation.
- **We:** team trust, psychological safety, shared norms, relationships, conflict style.
- **Its:** roles, decision rights, team structure, tools, workload, incentives.

**Typical blind spots:** the leader's shadow (UL) projected onto the team; lack of
explicit decision rights (LR) blamed on "poor communication" (LL).

## Product, design and startups

- **I:** user's needs, emotions, jobs-to-be-done, mental models; founders' vision and
  anxieties.
- **It:** user behaviors, usage data, the product's features and performance, team
  execution.
- **We:** user community and culture, brand meaning, team culture, values shared with
  users.
- **Its:** business model, market, competitors, channels, pricing, technology stack,
  regulation, platform dynamics.

**Typical blind spots:** analytics-only (UR) product decisions that miss *why* users
behave as they do (UL, LL); founders' unprocessed fear (UL) driving pivots.

## Marketing and communication

- Segment the audience by **level and type**, not only demographics.
- Map what each segment values (see the translation guide in
  [levels-and-lines.md](levels-and-lines.md)).
- Check the **LL**: what meaning does the brand carry in each community?
- Check the **LR**: channels, algorithms, media systems.

## Health and wellbeing

| Quadrant | Look at |
|---|---|
| **I** | Beliefs about health, motivation, emotions, stress, meaning, relationship to the body |
| **It** | Biology, symptoms, diet, sleep, exercise, medication, genetics, lab values |
| **We** | Family and cultural attitudes to food and health, social support, relationship with clinicians |
| **Its** | Healthcare system, insurance, access, work schedule, environment, food availability, income |

**Typical blind spot:** treating only UR (body, medication) while motivation (UL),
family habits (LL) and environment (LR) keep recreating the problem. Always recommend
qualified medical care for medical issues.

## Personal life and decisions

- **I:** What do I really want? What am I afraid of? What does this mean to me?
- **It:** What am I actually doing? Energy, health, skills, money behaviors?
- **We:** Who is affected? What do my close relationships and my culture expect?
- **Its:** Finances, time, location, job market, legal and practical constraints.

For decisions: score each option in all four quadrants, then ask which option the
*future self* who has grown one level would choose.

## Relationships and family

- **I:** each person's needs, wounds, attachment patterns, values.
- **It:** concrete behaviors, time spent together, communication habits, health.
- **We:** shared meaning, rituals, trust, intimacy, family culture, conflict patterns.
- **Its:** finances, housing, workload, schedules, children's needs, extended family
  systems.

**Look for:** level differences (e.g. one partner Amber-traditional, another
Green-egalitarian) and type differences (agency vs. communion) that are being fought as
moral issues.

## Education and learning

- **I:** learner's motivation, curiosity, self-concept, emotions about learning.
- **It:** skills, study behaviors, cognitive development, attention, sleep.
- **We:** classroom culture, peer norms, teacher–student relationship, family attitudes.
- **Its:** curriculum, assessment system, resources, schedules, technology, school
  policy.

Match teaching to the learner's level: concrete rules and structure for earlier
stages, autonomy and inquiry for later stages.

## Politics, policy and society

- **I:** citizens' beliefs, fears, hopes, identities.
- **It:** voting and consumption behaviors, health outcomes, crime, demographics.
- **We:** shared narratives, religion, cultural identities, social trust, polarization.
- **Its:** institutions, laws, economy, media systems, infrastructure, geopolitics.

**Integral politics** sees each political tradition as partly right: the
interior-focused (personal responsibility, values) and the exterior-focused
(systemic causes, structures); the traditional (Amber order), the modern (Orange
freedom and markets), the postmodern (Green justice and inclusion). Policies work best
when they are legible to several levels at once.

## Ecology and sustainability

- **I:** environmental values, eco-anxiety, sense of connection with nature.
- **It:** consumption behaviors, emissions, biology and ecology of the ecosystem.
- **We:** cultural attitudes to nature, community norms, environmental movements.
- **Its:** energy systems, economics, regulation, supply chains, technology.

**Typical blind spot:** treating ecology as only an LR/UR technical problem, or only as
an LL values problem. People at different levels care about nature for different
reasons (stewardship, efficiency, justice, systemic resilience) — use all of them.

## Technology and AI adoption

- **I:** users' and employees' fears, hopes, trust, sense of meaning in work.
- **It:** skills, usage behaviors, productivity, actual model performance.
- **We:** team norms about AI use, ethics, trust, identity of professions.
- **Its:** data infrastructure, governance, security, workflows, cost, regulation,
  vendor ecosystem.

**Typical blind spot:** rolling out tools (LR) and training (UR) without addressing
identity and fear (UL) or team norms (LL) — leading to quiet non-adoption.

## Conflict resolution (Bridge mode)

1. **Steelman each side:** what is each side *right* about? Which quadrant does each
   side emphasize?
2. **Identify levels:** are the sides arguing from different value systems?
3. **Identify types:** is this agency vs. communion, or a style clash?
4. **Identify states:** is anyone in threat mode? De-escalate first.
5. **Find the shared higher value** that both can recognize.
6. **Design agreements in all four quadrants:** personal commitments (I), concrete
   behaviors (It), shared understanding (We), structural changes (Its).
