# The Eight Zones — Integral Methodological Pluralism

Each quadrant can be looked at **from its own inside** or **from its own outside**. That
gives eight "zones", each with methods that bring forth real, different data. Use this
when the question is *"how would we actually know?"* — research design, discovery,
evaluation, due diligence, measuring change.

| Zone | Quadrant | View | Classic methods | Practical methods |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | UL — I | Inside of the inside | Phenomenology, introspection, meditation | Journaling, first-person reflection, felt-sense check-ins |
| 2 | UL — I | Outside of the inside | Structuralism, developmental psychology | Developmental assessments (e.g. sentence-completion tests), psychometrics, coaching interviews |
| 3 | LL — We | Inside of the we | Hermeneutics, dialogue | Deep interviews, story circles, shared sense-making, ethnographic immersion |
| 4 | LL — We | Outside of the we | Cultural anthropology, ethnomethodology, genealogy | Culture surveys, discourse analysis, analysis of rituals and norms |
| 5 | UR — It | Inside of the it | Autopoiesis, cognitive science, biological phenomenology | How an organism or individual system regulates itself; user mental-model studies |
| 6 | UR — It | Outside of the it | Empiricism, behaviorism, neuroscience | Behavioral analytics, A/B tests, biometrics, time-and-motion studies |
| 7 | LR — Its | Inside of the its | Social autopoiesis (Luhmann) | How the system sees itself: its own codes, reports, feedback loops |
| 8 | LR — Its | Outside of the its | Systems theory, ecology, economics | System dynamics, network analysis, market data, KPIs, supply-chain maps |

## How to use

1. **Name the question.** "Why are users churning?"
2. **Check which zones current evidence comes from.** Usually only Zone 6 (behavioral
   analytics) and Zone 8 (market data).
3. **Add at least one method per quadrant**, preferring one inside view and one outside
   view: e.g. user diaries (Zone 1), interviews (Zone 3), community norms analysis
   (Zone 4).
4. **Triangulate.** Where zones agree, confidence rises. Where they disagree, you have
   found the real question.

## The three strands of valid knowing (in every zone)

1. **Injunction** — the practice: "If you want to know this, do this."
2. **Apprehension** — the direct data or experience the practice brings forth.
3. **Confirmation / rejection** — checking with a community of people who have done the
   same practice competently.

A claim from a zone should only be rejected by people who have done that zone's
injunction. You cannot refute meditation reports without meditating, nor a clinical
trial without reading the data.
